# Voice Card & Case Studies Brief

**Intern:** Dave Andrei Almia Gallo · Backend AI Engineer @ FlyRank · Week 1

## Why it matters

A portfolio is mostly the framing around the work, and the framing is what makes a stranger trust you or scroll past. AI means you never face a blank page again, but only if you use it to get your decisions and results out, not to borrow a generic story.

## Brief

1. Write a **voice card** — five to seven words for how you want to sound (e.g. "direct, warm, plain, no buzzwords") — and add it to your Claude Project as a standing instruction.
2. Be interviewed: pick each real piece of work (start with what you're making in this internship) and have AI ask you about it, one question at a time, until the problem, your decisions, and the outcome are clear. Answer honestly and messily.
3. Draft each piece into a case study with three beats: **the problem**, **what you did (and what you decided)**, **what came of it** — plus bio and contact/CTA copy. Direct AI to use your words and keep your voice.
4. Edit hard. Read every line out loud; cut anything you wouldn't say or can't stand behind.

## Deliverable

The framed cases for every piece the sitemap calls for (one document) with your voice card at the top, plus one before/after: a generic AI line and your edited version.

## Pass / revise

- A framed case exists for each piece the sitemap calls for, not a bare screenshot or a title.
- Each case has the three beats and could only describe their project.
- It sounds like a specific person; the before/after shows the difference from generic AI copy.
- It speaks to the one audience and points at the one action; it's tight, no "results-driven" filler.
